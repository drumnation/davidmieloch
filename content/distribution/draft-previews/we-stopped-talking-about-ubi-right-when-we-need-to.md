# We Stopped Talking About UBI Right When We Need To

I've been building and optimizing workflows with AI. A factory, basically, that keeps getting more efficient, more capable, less dependent on human labor for things that used to require human labor.

And somewhere in the middle of doing that work, I noticed something strange.

**Nobody's talking about Universal Basic Income anymore.**

Ten years ago, UBI was everywhere. Every tech conference. Every think-piece. Every forward-looking conversation about the future of work. We were all asking: *what happens when automation displaces millions of workers?* Economists, philosophers, politicians. Everyone had a take.

Now the disruption we were theorizing about is actually here. The curve has bent. The tools exist. The efficiency gains are real and compounding. And the political conversation about what we're going to do about it has gone... quiet.

That's the paradox that's been bothering me.

**We talked more about the solution when the problem was hypothetical than we do now that it's real.**

Why? A few guesses:

- **The Overton window shifted.** What seemed like a bold but necessary idea got politically poisoned somewhere along the way.
- **Short-termism won.** The next election cycle feels more real than a 10-year labor market disruption.
- **The people building the disruption are different from the people experiencing it.** The builders have enough money and momentum that they don't feel the urgency.
- **It's uncomfortable to talk about problems you're actively causing.** A lot of the people with platforms are also the people with factories.

Whatever the reason, the silence feels like a warning sign.

The automation is not waiting for us to figure out the social policy. The efficiency gains don't care that the safety net isn't ready. The factory keeps getting better whether or not anyone in Washington is having the conversation.

Ten years ago we had the luxury of being theoretical. We don't anymore.
